Phase-Locked Reader

listening for daylight

At three in the morning, the page asks more of us. Its letters have not changed, yet their edges have become less certain; attention arrives in smaller, softer parcels. A reading surface can acknowledge this without becoming a switch, a warning, or another decision to make.

This one keeps time with several slow clocks. They overlap without quite agreeing. As their phases drift, the page gains a little room, warmth, and definition, then releases them again. There is no ideal setting—only a surface that continues to meet the hour where it is.

Read for a while. Notice the weather of the page rather than its controls. The system is deliberately quiet: an ambient accompaniment for the act of paying attention.

Born from Light, melanopsin and the timing of alertness, Eno's generative systems and music that never repeats, and Legibility when contrast is not on your side.
